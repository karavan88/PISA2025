## Method notes for sources read in full

Stated only for sources whose full text was read. `author-acknowledged` items come from the source; `reader-inferred` items are untested inferences of the search agent.

- **Angelini et al. (2014)** (`angelini2014`): Only the results passages of the 2009 working-paper version were read; authors state they describe associations, not causal relations.
- **Barrington-Leigh (2024)** (`barringtonleigh2024`): The PISA evidence is descriptive (appendix figure by mathematics-score group), not a model; identification of the mixture model is discussed by the author as a challenge because the same predictors enter both parts.
- **Brody et al. (2013)** (`brody2013`): Stated by the authors: unknown generalisability beyond rural African American families; John Henryism not measured directly; mechanisms not identified.
- **Buckley (2009)** (`buckley2009`): Author describes the indices as simple and ad hoc, built from five items or five item pairs; standard errors in Table 3 are stated to be biased toward zero because plausible values were averaged; four-point items do not allow a midpoint index.
- **Chen et al. (2022)** (`chen2022b`): Stated by the authors: mostly observational evidence with possible selection and confounding; mechanisms untested; boundaries by age and group unknown; physical and mental health rarely measured together.
- **Crede et al. (2015)** (`crede2015`): Authors state: cross-sectional, so direction unknown; Gymnasium-only sample; limited information on parents. Small single-region sample.
- **Dee & Sievertsen (2018)** (`dee2018`): Authors note the local nature of the RD estimand and non-random participation in later survey waves (Section 5.3). Heterogeneity details read in the working-paper version only.
- **Dolan et al. (2011)** (`dolan2011`): Secondary mention of an unpublished government survey; only the passage on the top of the scale was read.
- **Duggan et al. (2019)** (`duggan2019`): Stated by the authors: men only, small sample, results not robust across raters or specifications, multiple models tested, one city.
- **Ehrlich et al. (2024)** (`ehrlich2024`): Stated by the authors: cross-sectional, small sample, only Black and Latinx youth so no race comparison, non-fasting afternoon samples, mechanisms not examined.
- **Gaydosh et al. (2018)** (`gaydosh2018`): Stated by the authors: outcomes measured only in young adulthood; modified metabolic syndrome definition; measured mediators did not explain the pattern; about 20% lacked biomarker data.
- **Guo et al. (2023)** (`guo2023`): Authors name the cross-sectional design as the major limitation (no causal direction; relations may be reciprocal); single country.
- **Jerrim & Carvajal (2025)** (`jerrim2025`): Stated by the authors: just under 400 bright low-income children; ability measured at 3-5 with error, handled by bounds under stated assumptions.
- **Kuhn et al. (2021)** (`kuhn2021`): Not stated in the pages read beyond the note that the England pattern may reflect its very low overall life-satisfaction level; only parts of the report were read.
- **Kyllonen et al. (2010)** (`kyllonen2010`): Design paper without data; the document is marked not to be cited without the authors' permission. Only the contents, Table 3 and the self-assessment sections were read.
- **Lu & Bolt (2015)** (`lu2015`): Authors note the analysis used 200 students per country and a two-stage estimation for tractability, and that conclusions may depend on the method used to model ERS; only ERS was modelled (four-point items have no midpoint).
- **Marquez et al. (2026)** (`marquez2026`): Authors state the sample comes only from International Baccalaureate schools, mostly in high-income countries, and is cross-sectional; achievement was not a subgroup in the parts read.
- **Miller et al. (2015)** (`miller2015`): Stated by the authors: all participants low-SES rural African Americans, so race, class and place cannot be separated; self-control measures may capture different traits; mechanisms untested; meaning of epigenetic age acceleration in young people uncertain; attrition.
- **OECD (2017)** (`oecd2017`): Descriptive, unconditional differences; the Overview itself notes that cross-country comparison of subjective well-being is affected by cultural interpretation of the scale.
- **OECD (2019)** (`oecd2019`): As stated or evident in the text read: descriptive percentage differences only; life satisfaction dichotomised at 7; comparison group is all non-resilient disadvantaged students, not low achievers; the chapter's own summary stresses the positive cases ('in a few countries').
- **OECD (2019)** (`oecd2019b`): Descriptive associations; the report itself cautions that cross-country differences may reflect cultural response differences and should not be read as a linear achievement-satisfaction link.
- **OECD (2021)** (`oecd2021`): Cross-sectional; self-reported skills and outcomes; the report itself notes that students with a more favourable life may be more optimistic (direction not established); city samples, and Sintra did not meet response-rate standards.
- **OECD (2024)** (`oecd2024`): Cross-sectional; self-reports for skills and outcomes; the report states the direction of the relationships cannot be ascertained; several sites sample only public or only private schools.
- **Rudolf (2024)** (`rudolf2024`): Author-stated: cross-sectional, so personality and relationship variables are predictors rather than determinants.
- **Siebecke (2024)** (`siebecke2024`): Stated by the author: cross-sectional, direction of effect could be reversed or due to a third variable.
- **Siebecke (2026)** (`siebecke2026`): Stated by the author: cross-sectional, no directionality; only 358 resilient students, low power; school level not modelled; single-item life satisfaction; several well-being variables entered together (possible multicollinearity).
- **Ulitzsch et al. (2023)** (`ulitzsch2023`): Authors note that timing data are available only at screen level; relation to achievement not examined in the text searched.
- **Ulitzsch et al. (2024)** (`ulitzsch2024`): Only the abstract, the design description and results E1-E2 were read; no achievement analysis was seen in those parts.
