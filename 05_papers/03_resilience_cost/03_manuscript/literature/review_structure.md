# Structure of the background section

Written on 15 November 2026 to rebuild section 2 of the paper ("Background and research questions") from the existing evidence, following the author's six prompts for a literature review (scope, search plan, evidence matrix, thematic modules, outline, audit). No new search was run: the search plan and logs are in `scoping_review.md` and `search_logs/`, and the evidence matrix is `evidence_table.md` (one row per source and scoping question; columns Finding, Bearing, Read, Key). Every entry below names the block of the matrix and the key of the row it rests on. Strength: A = meta-analysis, systematic review or multi-country data, read in full; B = one large study, or a source read as an abstract only; C = small, qualitative or descriptive without controls. The rating follows the design and the reading depth recorded in the matrix.

## 1. Scope

**Position.** How do disadvantaged students who achieve highly rate their lives, compared with the disadvantaged schoolmates they have outperformed?

**Boundaries.** The object is the life satisfaction of 15-year-olds in the bottom status quartile and the top achievement quartile of their system. The methods in the literature are cross-sectional comparisons, cohort studies and adult panel studies of mobility. The candidate mechanisms are a non-linear relation of achievement with life satisfaction, masking by skills and relationships, the strain of achieving from a poor background, and comparison with schoolmates. Results are small and of mixed sign. The unresolved controversy is whether disadvantaged achievers pay any cost in self-reported wellbeing: the skin-deep-resilience studies say no, the inverted U and the OECD average say yes.

**Included.** Achievement and life satisfaction among adolescents; what resilient students have more of and how it relates to life satisfaction; whether a cost of achievement differs by family background; school context, sex, aspirations and skills as sources of variation.

**Left out.** The physical-health cost of resilience beyond one paragraph; adult mobility studies beyond the few that test life satisfaction; qualitative work on class identity; the measurement literature, which is treated as a threat in section 4.2 and not as a question.

**New angle.** A within-school comparison with disadvantaged low achievers, with a magnitude, in 68 systems, with skills held fixed.

**Maturity.** Still open. The number of comparable reviews in the last three years is unverified.
