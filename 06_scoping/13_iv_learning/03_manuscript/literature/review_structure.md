# Structure of the background section

Written on 23 November 2026 for section 2 of the paper ("Background and research questions"), following the author's six prompts for a
literature review (scope, search plan, evidence matrix, thematic modules, outline, audit) and the layout approved for paper 03.
Sources: `evidence_table.md`, `references.bib`, `search_logs/2026-11-23_search_log.md`.

## 1. Scope

**Position.** What does a year of school add at age 15, and does it add the same to everyone?

**Boundaries.** The object is the achievement of 15-year-olds and the gain from one more grade, by family socio-economic status, by
school, along the achievement distribution, and for socio-emotional skills. The methods in the literature are seasonal comparisons,
exposure designs built on birth and test dates, school-entry cutoffs, value-added models and admission lotteries.

**Included.** When the socio-economic gap opens; whether schooling changes it; how schools differ in level and in what they add; whether
those who know more gain more; what schooling does to socio-emotional skills and what skills do for learning.

**Left out.** Early childhood programmes; tracking and school choice as policies; teacher effects beyond two sentences; the long-run
effects of entry age on earnings; the measurement of socio-economic status. Relative age and season of birth are treated as threats in
section 4, not as background.

**New angle.** One entry-rule design applied to 34 systems at age 15: the socio-economic gap expressed in years of learning, and the gain
from a year by family background, by school, along the distribution and for skills. A cross-national test of whether schooling changes the
gap exists for grades 4 and 8 (`jensen2025`, a preprint, from test timing); it finds narrowing, where this paper finds no difference.

**Maturity.** Active and unsettled. The closest studies are from 2021 to 2026, five of them preprints. The closest of all surfaced only
when a failed query was rerun, so the claim that the combination is new rests on this search only.

## 3. Outline of section 2

Target 1,300 words. One subsection per module, one research question at the end of each.

| 2.5 Skills | 240 | What does school add to skills, and do skills change the gain? | Skills matter for levels; what a year adds to them, and through them, is open; self-reports need care. | Module 5 | Closes the section: a second outcome and a possible channel. | None. |

**Research questions, as they would close each subsection.**

1. How large is the socio-economic gap in achievement at 15 when measured in years of learning?
2. Does the gain from a year of school differ by family socio-economic status?
3. How much do schools of the same system differ in what a year adds, and do schools with a disadvantaged intake add less?
4. Does a year of school add the same along the achievement distribution?
5. What does a year of school add to socio-emotional skills, and do skills change who gains?

Approved by the author on 23 November 2026. Questions 1 and 2 are the project's earlier first question split in two; question 5
gathers the skills results.
